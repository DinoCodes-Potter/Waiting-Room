four steps, then you can leave

  1. Select a region

    Choose Watch a region… from the menu bar. Your screens dim and you drag a rectangle around whatever is slow.

  2. Choose when to alert you

    Pick whether you want to hear when it stops, starts or changes at all, and how still it has to be.

  3. Go do something else

    Waiting Room checks the region every second or two, so keep it visible on screen and get on with something else.

  4. Get the verdict

    It reads the text in the region and tells you whether it looks like a success or an error, with the last lines it saw.

three ways to be told

Every watch has its own mode, so one region can tell you when it finishes while another tells you when it wakes up.

Tell me when it's done.

For builds, installs, exports and long-running agents. Waiting Room waits for activity to begin, then alerts you once the region has stayed still for the time you set, from 2 to 60 seconds.

Tell me when it wakes up.

For a queue that's empty, a page that's about to update or a process that hasn't started. You get one alert the instant something in the region changes.

Tell me every time.

For a chat window, a log tail or a dashboard. Every change triggers an alert, with a three-second cooldown so you aren't buried.

small things that make it calm

Ignores blinking cursors
A pixel only counts if it differs from both of the previous two frames, so terminal cursors and other blinkers don't set it off.
Watches several things at once
Add as many regions as you like, across every connected display. Each has its own name, mode and sensitivity.
Catches slow progress
A progress bar that only inches forward still counts as activity. Choose High, Medium or Low sensitivity to suit the region.
Reads the screen for you
When a watch fires, on-device text recognition reads the region and the alert includes the last couple of lines.
Doesn't waste battery
It pauses while your screen sleeps or your session is locked, and checks less often in Low Power Mode.
Never watches itself
Waiting Room leaves its own windows out of every capture, so its banners can't trigger a watch.

your screen stays on your mac.

Waiting Room makes no network calls. Each screenshot is analyzed on your Mac and then discarded, and text recognition runs on-device with Apple's Vision framework.

You don't have to take our word for it. The whole app is seven Swift files, small enough to read over lunch. Read the source. This website has no analytics, no cookies and no third-party scripts either.

install in a minute

Download for macOS

Version 0.1, 1.1 MB. Needs an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later.

  1. Open the downloaded WaitingRoom.dmg and drag Waiting Room into Applications.
  2. Open it. The hourglass appears in your menu bar.
  3. Choose Grant Screen Recording access…, allow it in System Settings, then quit and reopen Waiting Room.

If macOS says it can't open the app

Waiting Room isn't notarized yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open the app once, dismiss the warning, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.

questions

Why does it need Screen Recording access?

macOS requires that permission for any app that captures part of the screen. Waiting Room uses it only to compare the pixels in the regions you choose, and nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Why isn't it notarized?

Not yet. Until it is, macOS shows a warning on first launch. You can verify the checksum above, read the source, or build it yourself with swift build.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

The download is built for Apple silicon only.

Is it free?

Yes. Waiting Room is open source under the MIT licence.
